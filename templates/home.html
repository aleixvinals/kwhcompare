How the comparison works

  1. We read your bill

    Your kWh, total amount due, utility and ESI ID or account number are pulled from the bill or the numbers you enter.

  2. We find your real rate

    Total ÷ kWh gives your all-in price per kWh, which we place against your state's average.

  3. We show cheaper options

    In deregulated states that means competitive supply plans. Elsewhere it means time-of-use rates, community solar and efficiency.

Can you choose your electricity provider?

In about a third of the country, yes. In deregulated markets your local utility still owns the poles and wires and still answers outage calls, but a retail provider sells you the electricity itself. That supply charge is usually more than half your bill, and it's the part you can shop.

Full retail choice: Texas, Pennsylvania, Ohio, Illinois, New York, New Jersey, Maryland, Massachusetts, Connecticut, Delaware, Maine, New Hampshire, Rhode Island and Washington D.C.

Limited choice: Michigan, Virginia, California, Oregon and Nevada allow switching only for some customers or through capped programs.

If you've never switched, you're probably on your utility's default "price to compare" or standard offer. Competitive fixed-rate plans often beat it, but always check the contract length, early cancellation fee and whether the rate is fixed or variable.

What if your state is regulated?

You can't change supplier, but you can still lower the bill:

Time-of-use rates. Most large utilities now offer cheaper overnight and weekend pricing. Running the dishwasher, laundry and EV charging off-peak can cut 5–15%.

Community solar. Subscribe to a local solar farm and get bill credits worth more than you pay, usually 5–15% savings with no panels on your roof.

Efficiency. A smart thermostat, LED bulbs and sealing drafts are the cheapest kilowatt-hours you'll ever buy.

How to read your electric bill

Every U.S. utility formats its bill differently, but the numbers that matter for comparing plans are always there. Find these four and you can compare any offer in under a minute.

Your real rate is the total amount due divided by kWh used. Advertised plan rates often exclude delivery charges, so compare all-in numbers whenever you can.

kWh used
Usually shown as "Energy used" or "Usage" with a meter read for this and last period.
Supply or generation charge
The electricity itself. In choice states this is the part a new provider replaces.
Delivery or TDU charge
What the utility charges to move power to your home. It stays the same whoever your supplier is.
ESI ID (Texas) or account / POD number
The ID of your meter. Providers need it to switch you; in Texas the first digits show which utility serves you.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average electricity rate in the US in 2026?

About 18.3 cents per kWh for homes as of mid-2026, up roughly 5% from a year earlier. Nevada, Nebraska and Utah are the cheapest; Hawaii, California and much of New England are the most expensive.

Is it worth switching electricity providers?

In deregulated states, households on the default utility rate often save 10–20% on the supply portion by moving to a competitive fixed-rate plan. Watch for teaser rates, monthly fees and cancellation fees.

What is an ESI ID and where do I find it?

The Electric Service Identifier is a 17- or 22-digit number unique to your meter in deregulated Texas. It's printed on your bill near your service address. The first digits identify your utility, such as CenterPoint or Oncor.

How do I calculate my cost per kWh?

Divide your total bill by the kWh you used. $182 ÷ 1,000 kWh = 18.2¢ per kWh. This all-in figure is the fairest way to compare plans.

Will switching cause a power outage?

No. Your utility keeps delivering power over the same lines. Only the company that bills you for supply changes.

Do you store my bill?

No. The numbers you type are calculated in your browser and are never sent to us or stored.
